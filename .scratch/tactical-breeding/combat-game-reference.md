# Turn-based grid combat — game reference checklist

Research date: 2026-11-06. Status: comparative research, **not adopted game-design requirements**.

Scope: combat rules; controllable combat units and their composition; how those units enter the player's roster or battlefield. Story, presentation, artwork, animation, and unrelated progression/economy are excluded. Recruitment triggered by campaign events is mentioned only because it determines unit acquisition.

**Reading the checklist:** `- [ ]` marks a feature for later examination, not an implementation task, recommendation, or claim that we have playtested it. Each row separates **Combat**, **Units**, and **Acquisition**. Acquisition distinguishes permanent roster additions from temporary summons and battlefield production. Square grids are the default unless marked **hex** or **gridless**. Isometric presentation does not mean a different grid topology.

**Versions and evidence:** This is a core-mechanics survey, not an exhaustive rules manual or a verification of every current patch. Named editions take precedence over series-wide generalizations. Sources are linked within each row: **P** = primary developer/publisher/manual; **C** = community reference/guide; **S** = secondary reporting. Some older games require community documentation. Exact damage formulas, recruitment probabilities, and exhaustive equipment/ability lists are deliberately outside scope. No commercial-success ranking is implied.

## 1. Ankama games

French-language references are used here. DOFUS means the main PC game, not Retro or Touch; WAKFU's multi-character options depend on server rules. WAVEN is explicitly a historical early-access reference so that old rules and announced redesigns are not combined.

## 5. Cover, attrition and stealth squads

Armor, health, morale, injuries, elevation, surrounding and zones of control interact.<br>- [ ] **Units — composition:** Procedurally differentiated mercenaries with background, traits, base attributes and growth potential; level-up attributes/perks and equipment define roles without mandatory fixed classes.<br>- [ ] **Equipment mechanics:** Weapons supply different actions and armor interactions; heavy gear affects fatigue. Hiring cost, wages and uncertainty about a recruit are acquisition constraints.<br>- [ ] **Persistence:** Maintain active and reserve brothers; injuries/death can remove or compromise individuals. No ordinary battlefield production or general monster capture system.<br>Sources: [developer combat/recruitment/build description, P][brothers-store]. |
| **Invisible, Inc.** | - [ ] **Combat:** Side-based tile-grid stealth; agent action points, facing/vision, cover, doors and sound determine safe movement. Security alarm escalation makes time costly.<br>- [ ] **Actions/resources:** Knock out/pin or evade guards, ambush, hack devices through Incognita using shared power; weapons/items can have ammunition, charge or cooldown limitations. Elimination is usually not the objective.<br>- [ ] **Units — composition:** Agents have individual starting traits/augmentations plus upgradeable skills, limited inventory and acquired equipment. Incognita's programs are a shared support layer, not another moving agent.<br>- [ ] **Acquisition:** Choose starting agents from unlocked options; rescue additional operatives from detention missions, subject to roster limits.<br>- [ ] **Persistence:** Develop the team over a run; campaign unlocks broaden future starting choices. Rescuing an agent differs from purchasing gear or hacking a temporary device.<br>Sources: [developer description, P][invisible-store]; [campaign/mechanics guide, C][invisible-guide]. |

## 6. Armies, production and creature stacks

These are larger-scale references. A unit can represent a troop type or a stack rather than one individually customized creature.

| Game titles | Features checklist |
| --- | --- |
| **The Battle for Wesnoth** | - [ ] **Combat:** **Hex-grid**, side-based turns; movement points and an attack per activation. Terrain controls defense/movement; time of day modifies alignment-dependent damage; hit chance, resistances and retaliation shape matchups.<br>- [ ] **Units — composition:** Faction/type + level + traits + movement/resistance profile + melee/ranged attacks with damage types and specials. Experience can lead to branching advancement.<br>- [ ] **Acquisition:** A leader recruits from a permitted list at a keep/castle using gold; villages support income and upkeep. Scenarios can also provide special joins.<br>- [ ] **Persistence:** Campaigns commonly allow paid **recall** of surviving veterans; recruitment of a new unit and recall of an existing one are distinct.<br>- [ ] **Boundary:** Campaign recruitment lists and special rules vary; the core game does not require every scenario to offer the same units or advancement paths.<br>Sources: [official project/manual entry, P][wesnoth-manual]; [project overview, P][wesnoth-project]. |
| **Advance Wars — first game / Re-Boot Camp's AW1 campaign** | - [ ] **Combat:** Side-based grid turns; move and act; terrain defense, fog of war, transports and direct-versus-indirect fire matter. Unit damage generally decreases with remaining health.<br>- [ ] **Resources:** Units have fuel and ammunition; captured properties generate funds and supply services. Capturing HQ or destroying the opposing force can decide battles, with scenario-specific alternatives.<br>- [ ] **Units — composition:** Fixed military unit types with health, movement mode, attack ranges, ammunition and matchup strengths; the commanding officer provides army-level modifiers and a charged CO Power.<br>- [ ] **Acquisition:** Produce units during battles at appropriate bases/airports/ports by paying funds; some missions instead provide a fixed force.<br>- [ ] **Persistence:** Ordinary units do not form a persistent, individually leveled campaign roster; available COs are a separate unlock/selection layer.<br>Sources: [Nintendo remake overview, P][aw-nintendo]; [unit/rules reference, C][aw-wiki]. |
| **Advance Wars 2: Black Hole Rising / Re-Boot Camp's AW2 campaign** | - [ ] **Combat:** Retains AW1's grid turns, terrain, capture, direct/indirect fire, supply and health-dependent combat.<br>- [ ] **Units — composition:** Expanded unit/CO roster; officers have both normal and stronger **Super CO Powers**, adding timing choices to army-wide advantages.<br>- [ ] **Acquisition:** Battlefield production using property income; map-defined starting units and mission restrictions; CO access/unlocks remain separate from buying troops.<br>- [ ] **Boundary:** Do not import later games' mechanics, such as Dual Strike's paired-CO system, into AW2.<br>Sources: [Nintendo remake overview, P][aw-nintendo]; [series edition/unit documentation, C][aw-wiki]. |
| **Wargroove** | - [ ] **Combat:** Side-based grid turns; capture towns for income, recruit at production structures and exploit terrain, range and transport.<br>- [ ] **Units — composition:** Defined troop types with health, movement and attack matchups; each type has a specific condition for a guaranteed critical hit. Factions largely share corresponding ordinary unit mechanics.<br>- [ ] **Commanders:** A deployed commander is a powerful battlefield unit with a distinct charged **Groove**; commander choice is not merely a cosmetic faction label.<br>- [ ] **Acquisition:** Spend funds on units at appropriate structures during battle; scenarios also supply starting armies. Commanders are selected/unlocked separately.<br>- [ ] **Persistence:** Standard skirmish troops are battle-local rather than a persistent individually trained roster.<br>Sources: [developer gameplay guide, P][wargroove-play]; [unit conditions/stats, C][wargroove-units]. |
| **Wargroove 2** | - [ ] **Combat:** Retains grid-based army turns, capture/income, production, terrain and conditional unit criticals; commanders can charge stronger **Supercharged Grooves**.<br>- [ ] **Units — composition:** Fixed troop archetypes plus distinctive commanders; army composition and battlefield positioning are central. Collectible items can grant an ordinary unit special, Groove-like abilities, adding individual development within the relevant mode.<br>- [ ] **Acquisition — campaign/skirmish:** Buy units at battlefield production structures where the map permits.<br>- [ ] **Acquisition — Conquest:** Start a run with an army and add recruits through the run's opportunities/events; surviving troops persist between encounters and lost troops are not automatically replaced.<br>- [ ] **Mode boundary:** Conquest's persistent recruitment/attrition loop must not be generalized to every skirmish or campaign map.<br>Sources: [developer Conquest explanation, P — development-era design article][wargroove-conquest]; [release description, P][wargroove2-store]. |
| **Heroes of Might and Magic III — added stack-combat reference** | - [ ] **Combat:** Separate **hex-grid** battle arenas; creature-stack turns depend largely on speed, with wait/defend and retaliation rules. Heroes support combat with spells rather than normally occupying a moving combat tile themselves.<br>- [ ] **Units — composition:** A battlefield piece is a **stack of identical creatures**: count + creature-type health, attack/defense, damage, speed, shots and special abilities. Hero stats, skills, artifacts and spells modify the army.<br>- [ ] **Army structure:** A hero carries up to seven stacks. Splitting/merging changes the number of activations and distribution of damage without inventing a new creature species.<br>- [ ] **Acquisition:** Recruit creatures from towns and dwellings, constrained by growth availability and resource cost; some neutral groups can join. Certain abilities/spells can also create or recover creatures.<br>- [ ] **Persistence:** Creature casualties reduce stack counts across encounters; heroes and surviving armies persist on the strategic map.<br>Sources: [original publisher manual hosted with the Steam edition, P][heroes-manual]. |
| **Warlock: Master of the Arcane — broader strategic reference** | - [ ] **Combat:** Turn-based **hex-grid** fighting directly on the world map rather than a separate squad arena; terrain, movement, ranges, resistances and global spell support shape engagements.<br>- [ ] **Units — composition:** Unit type/race with stats, damage/resistance profile, abilities and acquired perks/upgrades; special lord units have additional equipment options in the relevant supported content.<br>- [ ] **Acquisition:** Train units through city buildings; summon units with spells; special resources/settlements unlock additional troop types. Availability is tied to territorial and building access.<br>- [ ] **Persistence:** Units survive and develop across world-map turns; losing them is an army loss, not just a temporary summon expiring by default.<br>- [ ] **Scope boundary:** Useful for acquisition/unit variety, but its 4X city layer is not a requirement for our combat design.<br>Sources: [publisher description and content distinctions, P][warlock-store]; [terrain/combat reference, C][warlock-combat]. |

## Evidence limitations and follow-up boundaries

- These are sourced desk-research summaries, not hands-on combat acceptance tests. The checkboxes remain available for later examination/selection.
- Publisher/store descriptions establish broad features but often omit turn-order details and recruitment rules; community guides are explicitly labeled where used. A first-party domain's user forum is still community evidence, not developer confirmation.
- Several pages were not directly readable: the WAVEN chest wiki returned 403; the Brigandine systems page returned maintenance; the Square Enix systems page extraction was incomplete. Search-index evidence was used for those narrow points and is not equivalent to a full manual audit.
- WAVEN's readily available French combat article includes **alpha-era** material. It is used as historical evidence only; no current deck-size, line-of-sight, equipment-slot or announced-refactor claim should be inferred from it. Pin a playable build before a deeper rules breakdown.
- Broad series suggestions were resolved into named reference editions rather than pretending one row can describe every release. Both members of explicitly suggested pairs are retained; additional references are Awakening, Brigandine and Heroes III.
- No combat mechanics have been selected for our game. The authoritative baseline remains [spec.md](./spec.md); this catalogue is supporting research only.
